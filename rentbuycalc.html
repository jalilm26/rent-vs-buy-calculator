Rent or Buy Calculator (Are You Better Off Buying or Renting in The Long-run?)

This calculator compares your net worth if you buy a home against renting the same space and investing the difference. It includes Ontario and Toronto land transfer tax, CMHC insurance and Canadian mortgage compounding.

Method and assumptions

  • Both households spend the same total each month. Whoever has the lower housing cost invests the difference, and the renter starts by investing the down payment and closing costs.
  • Mortgage uses Canadian semi-annual compounding, a fixed rate for the whole horizon and no prepayments.
  • Land transfer tax uses the Ontario and Toronto bracket schedules for 1–2 single-family homes (about $4,000 and $4,475 first-time buyer rebates). Toronto’s extra tiers above $3 million are not modelled. Check current rates with the Province of Ontario and the City of Toronto.
  • CMHC premiums: 4.00% (5–9.99% down), 3.10% (10–14.99%), 2.80% (15–19.99%), added to the mortgage. Only applies to homes up to $1.5 million.
  • No capital gains tax on the home (principal residence). Renter’s investments are untaxed if sheltered in a TFSA or RRSP, otherwise 50% of gains is deducted at the end as a rough estimate.
  • Property tax and maintenance follow the home’s value. Condo fees, insurance and rent grow at the rent growth rate. Renter’s insurance is $30 a month. Legal and other closing costs are $1,800.
  • This is a planning tool, not financial advice. The result depends heavily on future price growth and investment returns, which nobody knows.
